The documents required to file an income tax return depend on your sources of income. Gathering everything before you start makes the process faster and reduces the chance of an incomplete or incorrect return. Here is a general guide organised by taxpayer type.

For salaried individuals

  • Form 16 from your employer
  • Salary slips for the year, if available
  • Bank statements for all accounts held during the year
  • Details of any other income, such as bank interest or rent received
  • Proof of tax-saving investments and eligible deductions
  • Details of any advance tax or self-assessment tax paid

For freelancers and professionals

  • Records of professional receipts and fees earned during the year
  • Details of business expenses incurred, such as rent, internet, travel and professional fees paid
  • Bank statements for accounts used for professional income
  • Details of TDS deducted by clients, along with the relevant certificates
  • Advance tax payment details, where applicable

For business owners

  • Books of accounts, or at least a summary of sales, purchases and expenses for the year
  • Bank statements for all business accounts
  • Details of fixed assets purchased or sold during the year
  • GST returns filed during the year, for cross-reference
  • Loan statements, if applicable, showing interest paid
  • Details of any audit conducted, where applicable

If you have capital gains

  • Purchase and sale documents for property, shares, mutual funds or other capital assets
  • Broker or fund statements showing transaction-wise gains and losses
  • Details of any improvement costs or expenses connected to the transfer of an asset
  • Documents supporting any exemption being claimed on reinvestment

Organising your documents

However you file, it helps to keep documents organised under a few simple categories: income documents, expense and deduction proof, bank statements, and TDS or tax payment records. A simple folder structure, whether physical or digital, saved by financial year makes it much easier to find what you need, not just at filing time but also if a question comes up later. Where possible, keep digital copies as well, since these are easier to share and store securely, and are less likely to be lost or damaged.

If you're missing a document

It is common to be missing a certificate or statement when you first sit down to file. Most banks, employers and brokers can reissue statements or certificates on request, and many are also available for download from the relevant online portal. Rather than estimating figures or leaving out an income source because a document is missing, it is worth taking the time to obtain the correct paperwork first, since an incomplete or inaccurate return can lead to queries later.

When to start

It is best to start collecting documents well before the filing season rather than waiting until the last moment. This gives you time to request missing certificates from employers, clients or banks, reconcile TDS credits against your own records, and review your income and deductions carefully rather than filing in a hurry. Starting early also gives you room to plan tax payments in an orderly way rather than scrambling close to a deadline.
