Receiving a notice from the GST or income tax department can feel unsettling, but most notices simply ask for clarification, reconciliation or additional information. Reacting calmly and methodically usually leads to a smoother outcome than reacting in a hurry.

Notices come in different forms

Not every communication from the department signals a dispute. Some notices are simply a request for information, a reminder to reconcile a mismatch, or a routine query linked to a specific return. Others, such as a scrutiny notice or a show-cause notice, indicate that the department wants a more detailed explanation or is proposing a specific demand. Reading the notice carefully to understand which category it falls into is the first and most useful step, since it determines how much detail your response needs and how quickly you need to act.

1. Don't panic — read the notice carefully

Start by identifying what type of notice you have received and which section or provision it refers to. Notices can range from a simple request for information or reconciliation to a formal show-cause notice. Understanding what is actually being asked helps you respond to the right point rather than submitting unrelated information.

2. Note the deadline

Every notice will specify a timeline for response. Mark this date immediately and plan backward from it, allowing enough time to gather documents, reconcile figures and prepare a considered reply rather than a last-minute one. If a genuine extension is needed, this should be requested through the proper channel well before the deadline.

3. Gather the relevant records

Depending on the notice, this could include:

  • Sales and purchase invoices for the period in question
  • GST returns filed, along with reconciliation working files
  • Bank statements showing relevant payments or receipts
  • Books of accounts and ledgers for the period
  • Earlier correspondence with the department, if any
  • TDS certificates or income-tax computation working files, for income-tax notices

4. Reconcile the figures

Most notices relate to a mismatch or discrepancy of some kind, whether between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B, between GSTR-3B and GSTR-2B, or between income reported and information available with the department. Reconciling the figures yourself first helps you understand whether the notice reflects an actual error, a timing difference, or simply a data-matching issue that needs an explanation.

5. Respond through the portal, with documentation

Replies to most GST and income-tax notices are filed through the respective department portal. Keep your response factual, refer clearly to the specific point raised, and attach supporting documentation and reconciliation statements. A well-documented response is easier for the department to review and reduces the chance of repeated queries.

6. Keep a record of all correspondence

Save copies of the notice, your reply, acknowledgements and any further correspondence in one place. If the matter continues over multiple rounds, having a complete file makes it much easier to track and respond consistently.

When to seek help

If the notice involves a significant amount, an audit observation, a show-cause notice, or if you are unsure how to interpret the specific provision being cited, it is worth seeking professional assistance before responding. Responding incorrectly, or missing the point the department is actually raising, can lead to further correspondence and unnecessary delay, so it is often more efficient to have the notice reviewed properly before you reply.
